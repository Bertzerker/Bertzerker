## Hi, I’m Christoph.

I’m a trained IT systems electronics technician, now retraining in software development. I enjoy understanding how systems work—and finding ways to make hardware and software work together.

## A practical foundation in electronics and IT

I completed my apprenticeship as an IT systems electronics technician at Siemens in 2003, earning my IHK qualification. My subsequent experience includes planning, programming, installing and maintaining fire and intrusion detection systems, including cameras, intrusion sensors, acoustic sensors and all types of fire detectors.

I have also worked in high-current cable testing for heavy machinery, electrical testing and fault diagnosis. Alongside my professional work, I have continued building PCs, setting up home networks and helping family and friends with IT problems.

That background shapes how I approach a problem: understand the system, investigate the cause and check whether a change actually works.

## Building a new career in software development

A workplace injury and pre-existing health problems led me to change direction. Since June 2025, I have been retraining at the Deutsche Angestellten-Akademie in Gelsenkirchen as a *Fachinformatiker für Anwendungsentwicklung*—an IT specialist in application development.

The two-year programme includes a practical internship and a project for the IHK final examination. I want to apply what I’m learning in a development team, contribute to real projects and gain experience with everyday development processes.

## Learning by building and testing

I’m developing foundations in Python, C#, JavaScript, HTML/CSS and SQL, together with object-oriented programming, design patterns and software development principles. I work with Visual Studio and VS Code, and have experience with Windows, Windows Server, Linux and Docker.

Open-source software and hardware are a particular interest. In a retraining project, I experimented with automating a PiCrawler robot, adding accelerometer, gyroscope and LiDAR sensors, along with microswitches. The aim was to explore obstacle avoidance, orientation and detecting when the robot’s legs touch the ground.

I also use AI tools to support learning, code investigation and debugging. My PlayStation patch, MiSTer and microcontroller projects show how that assistance can be combined with live testing and verification.

## Where hardware meets software

My interests include microcontrollers, game programming, 3D printing, CNC and laser cutting. My projects bring those interests together: classic-game control patches, MiSTer experiments, controller firmware and a local LAN party platform.

I share the process as well as the results, including what has been tested and what still needs work.
